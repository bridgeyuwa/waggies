# Veterinary Service Scope Benchmarks for Waggies

*Research snapshot: 2026-10-23*

## Purpose and scope

This focused extension supports the Waggies competitive-strategy map by identifying veterinary service categories visible in public provider menus and official pet-travel requirements. It complements the broader [Nigeria coverage map](waggies-nigeria-coverage-map-2026-10.md) and [regional and international service benchmarks](waggies-international-service-benchmarks-2026-10.md); it does not repeat their provider inventories or overall coverage analysis.

Waggies owner-confirmed scope at this snapshot: veterinary support is for boarding guests and pets using Waggies import/export relocation services; veterinary appointments are offered daily from 9:00am to 5:00pm; a veterinarian is physically onsite overnight; broader access to other pets is planned for later. At the time this snapshot was prepared, the owner had not yet confirmed which individual clinical procedures, diagnostics, preventive services, or travel documents Waggies itself performs. On 2026-10-25, the owner confirmed that shampoo baths before pickup and nail trims during boarding are included when needed and appropriate for dogs and cats, and that anal gland expression is provided for either species when the veterinarian determines it is needed. Other procedures, diagnostics, preventive services, and travel documents remain unconfirmed. This report therefore provides a comparison menu, not a Waggies service claim or clinical recommendation.

Provider websites and association listings are evidence of what their publishers advertise or declare, not an independent audit of availability, quality, outcomes, capacity, or regulatory authority. A provider’s service menu should not be treated as a complete list of what all competitors offer.

## Service categories to consider for Waggies’ operating map

These categories are synthesized from the provider menus and official requirements below. They are prompts for owner capability classification, not recommendations to launch every service.

### 1. Boarding health and continuity

- Review existing health and vaccination records during boarding-request review; obtain missing records before staff confirms the request, as already decided in Waggies’ operating guardrails.
- A veterinary assessment when staff or the owner discloses a health concern, special-care need, or a travel-related question.
- A defined observation and escalation process during the stay, including who contacts the owner and where urgent care is obtained.
- Follow-up notes and continuity of care: record the presenting concern, examination or care provided, owner instructions, medication details, and any referral or follow-up action.

These practices align with publicly advertised boarding/clinic combinations in Abuja and Lagos, but the research cannot determine what Waggies currently records or performs.

### 2. Preventive care and routine treatment

Competitor menus commonly include examinations, vaccination, parasite-related care, consultations, treatment, and medication. The relevant operational categories to classify are:

- Routine physical or wellness examinations.
- Vaccination assessment and administration, with the species- and risk-appropriate schedule determined by Waggies’ veterinary lead.
- Flea, tick, worm, and other parasite assessment or treatment where the veterinary team provides it.
- Consultation and treatment for illness or injury.
- Medication review, prescribing/dispensing, and administration only to the extent the veterinary lead and staff can safely and lawfully provide.

WSAVA’s current dog and cat vaccination guidance is the professional reference point; competitor boarding requirements should not be copied as a universal vaccine schedule. Vaccine selection depends on veterinary assessment and regional/local factors. [WSAVA vaccination guidelines](https://wsava.org/Global-Guidelines/Vaccination-Guidelines/)

### 3. Diagnostics, procedures, and referral boundaries

Public menus in Nigeria and the US include laboratory diagnostics, X-ray, ultrasound, dentistry, and surgery. These services require specific equipment, expertise, patient selection, and aftercare. The operating map should distinguish:

- Tests performed onsite by Waggies.
- Tests collected onsite but processed by an external laboratory.
- Imaging or procedures performed onsite.
- Services referred to another clinic or hospital.
- Services Waggies does not currently offer.

The provider pages show that such menus exist in the market; they do not establish that Waggies has the equipment or operating arrangements to offer them. Do not market diagnostics or procedures until the veterinary lead confirms current delivery and any referral/partner handoff.

### 4. Import/export travel-health preparation

Travel cases may need a route-specific veterinary readiness and documentation workflow. Relevant tasks to classify include:

- Review the pet’s clinical history and vaccination records early enough to meet the destination timeline.
- Confirm identity and microchip number, read/scan it where available, and match it to the pet’s records.
- Arrange destination-required vaccination, treatment, laboratory testing, or rabies antibody/titer testing when applicable.
- Examine fitness for travel and identify health or welfare constraints, including whether air travel is appropriate for that animal.
- Prepare or coordinate health certificates and route-specific documents with the competent government authority and any appropriately accredited/authorized veterinarian.
- Track endorsements, inspection, permit, airline, and destination requirements with named owners and deadlines.

These are not universal requirements for every route. The destination, mode, carrier, and current official rules control. AVMA recommends a pre-travel veterinary examination and checking route-specific requirements; it notes that testing, health certificates, and the veterinarian’s accreditation can vary. [AVMA travel FAQ](https://avma.org/public/PetCare/Pages/Traveling-with-Your-Pet-FAQs.aspx)

Nigeria’s NAQS describes health inspection of imported/exported live animals, including dogs and cats, verification of permits, and certification of veterinary health certificates. Its export requirements page also describes export-permit application, inspection, possible lab analysis/certification, and quarantine/clearance. This means Waggies should distinguish its own veterinary work and coordination from statutory inspection, permits, endorsements, or certificates that belong to government authorities or specifically authorized professionals. [NAQS animal quarantine mandate](https://naqs.gov.ng/animal/), [NAQS export requirements](https://naqs.gov.ng/animal-export-requirements/)

Destination requirements can add steps. For example, Great Britain’s official guidance ties identification, rabies vaccination, timing, possible rabies blood tests, tapeworm treatment for dogs, and travel documents to the country of origin and destination rules. This is an example of route dependency, not advice that all those items apply to every Waggies relocation. [UK pet-entry overview](https://www.gov.uk/bring-pet-to-great-britain?cat=law), [rabies timing and record requirements](https://www.gov.uk/bring-pet-to-great-britain/rabies-vaccination-and-boosters)

## What the comparison suggests

1. **Make the integrated model legible.** Abuja providers and YappaVille publicly connect veterinary support with boarding, and some connect it with pet travel. Waggies’ currently confirmed audience—boarding guests and import/export pets—supports describing veterinary care as part of those journeys, subject to a confirmed service menu.
2. **Offer a clear tier of care before a broad clinic menu.** A safe first scope to document is records review, pre-travel readiness coordination, routine boarding health support, and case-by-case veterinary assessment—only where Waggies’ veterinary lead confirms each is delivered today. Clinical diagnostics and procedures belong in a separate “in-house / referred / not offered” inventory.
3. **Keep medical decisions with the veterinary lead.** Use WSAVA and route-specific authorities as references; do not copy a competitor’s vaccine list or promise a standard medication, diagnostic, or treatment package.
4. **Show the boundary between Waggies and third parties.** For relocation, say which steps Waggies’ veterinarian performs, which steps are coordinated with the owner’s vet, lab, airline, or regulator, and which remain government approvals or fees. This mirrors the useful transparency in relocation-coordinator models without claiming their regulatory authorizations.
5. **Use service records as a trust and continuity opportunity.** VCA and relocation-coordinator examples make records, timelines, handoffs, and follow-up more visible. The transferable idea is clarity and continuity, not copying a large hospital network’s app or staffing model.

| Routine exams and veterinary consultations | Boarding health concerns and baseline travel assessment |

| Parasite assessment/treatment | Boarding welfare and some route-specific requirements |
| Illness/injury assessment and treatment | Care escalation during boarding and pre-travel readiness |
| Medication prescription/dispensing and administration | Boarded-pet care; already approved for case-by-case review, but actual accepted medication scope remains to be set |
| Laboratory testing and sample handling | Diagnostics and route-required tests |
| Imaging, dentistry, surgery, hospitalization | Higher-capability procedures requiring explicit facility/staff/aftercare evidence |
| Travel fitness examination | Determines whether the individual pet is clinically appropriate for a proposed route |
| Microchip scan/implantation and identity-record verification | Identity continuity and destination requirements where applicable |
| Travel vaccination/titer tests, treatments, and health documents | Route-dependent preparation, with third-party and official-authority handoffs distinguished |
| Emergency response and referral after hours | Boarding and relocation continuity; onsite overnight veterinarian presence is owner-confirmed, but scope, escalation protocol, and facility capability still require confirmation |

## Evidence strength

- **Official government/professional sources:** high confidence for the published mandate or guidance, not for Waggies’ ability to perform the work.
- **Provider-owned menus:** medium confidence for what the provider advertises; not independent proof of current delivery or outcomes.
- **IPATA member listing:** medium confidence for member-declared service categories; not an audit of case quality. YappaVille is a Nairobi analogue only.
- **Waggies current capability:** owner-confirmed facts are limited to customer scope (boarding and relocation pets), daily appointment hours (9am–5pm), and a veterinarian onsite overnight. Individual veterinary procedures/services remain unconfirmed.
