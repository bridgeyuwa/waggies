# Waggies Nigeria Demand and Coverage Feasibility Signals

*Research snapshot: 2026-10-23*

## Purpose and limits

This note gathers publicly available evidence that may inform Waggies’ geographic choices for dog and cat boarding, veterinary care, and pet relocation. The user specified public sources only because Waggies has no customer base from which to collect customer evidence. No customer interviews, surveys, or first-party demand data are used here.

This is a feasibility scan, not a market-size estimate or a coverage recommendation. Public data reviewed does not support a reliable state-by-state estimate of owned pets, paid pet-care demand, willingness to pay, boarding occupancy, or relocation volume. Human population, airport presence, veterinary directories, and provider websites are screening signals only; none establishes demand or deliverable capacity.

## Findings

1. **The public evidence is not strong enough to promise or prioritize nationwide service coverage yet.** State-level dog population estimates are available for only a small share of Nigeria’s states, cat ownership data is especially thin, and public veterinary-premises counts are dated and not broken down geographically in the source reviewed. The decision should use service-specific phases and explicit confidence levels rather than treating all 36 states and the FCT as one market. [Abubakar et al., 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/zph.13084); [WOAH Nigeria veterinary-services evaluation, 2019](https://rr-africa.woah.org/app/uploads/2020/02/20190626_nigeria-pvs-fu-report_final-1.pdf)
2. **Population can help screen where to investigate, but cannot stand in for pet-care demand.** NBS’s 2023 demographic bulletin reports projected populations of 15.67 million for Kano, 13.71 million for Lagos, 10.66 million for Katsina, and about 3.17 million for the FCT. These are human-population projections, not counts of pet-owning households, target customers, or serviceable customers. [NBS Demographic Bulletin 2023](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/182/download/1392/Demographic%20Bulletin%202023.pdf)
3. **Existing dog studies show that ownership and dog-management patterns vary materially by location and study method.** A 2024 review found dog population/ownership/vaccination/bite data for only 6 of 37 states/FCT units reviewed and reported wide differences in published dog-to-human ratios. Its national dog-population estimate is an extrapolation, not a count of companion animals likely to use paid services. A Kwara household study found 20.1% of 1,460 surveyed households owned at least one dog, but it cannot be transferred to other states or used as a cat/boarding estimate. [Abubakar et al., 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/zph.13084); [Kwara dog-ecology study, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33611031/)
4. **Government veterinary infrastructure exists across the federation, but it does not establish equal private companion-animal access.** A 2019 WOAH evaluation described veterinary offices, state veterinary hospitals/clinics, and LGA veterinary units, while also reporting uneven staff distribution and states without federal professionals. It recorded 701 registered veterinary-practicing premises to date, including 168 registrations in 2018; the report does not provide a current state-by-state clinic inventory. The report also warned that the veterinarian register was stale at the time. Treat these figures as historical context, not current supply. [WOAH Nigeria veterinary-services evaluation, 2019](https://rr-africa.woah.org/app/uploads/2020/02/20190626_nigeria-pvs-fu-report_final-1.pdf)
5. **Relocation should be evaluated as a set of verified routes and handoffs, not a blanket national footprint.** FAAN lists domestic airports and international gateways, which signals transport infrastructure but does not confirm active schedules, animal acceptance, cargo handling, route reliability, or Waggies’ access to a carrier. For international movements, NAQS requires export-permit steps, inspection, certification, and clearance; requirements of destination countries and carriers also matter. A route should count as serviceable only after its actual provider, documentation, handoffs, and quote process are verified. [FAAN domestic airports](https://faan.gov.ng/local-airports/); [FAAN international airports](https://faan.gov.ng/international-airports/); [NAQS animal quarantine](https://naqs.gov.ng/animal/); [NAQS animal export requirements](https://naqs.gov.ng/animal-export-requirements/)

## What the evidence says by service line

| Service | Public signals that can help screen coverage | What they do not establish | Feasibility unit to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boarding | Local dog-ecology studies and named operating locations can indicate where companion-animal services are publicly visible. Population projections can identify places for further diligence. | Current demand for paid boarding, cat demand, occupancy, willingness to pay, safety quality, or ability to provide care outside a staffed/partner facility. | A specific city or service area attached to an inspected, staffed Waggies facility or verified boarding partner, with capacity and care standards confirmed. |
| Veterinary care | VCN/WOAH regulatory context, state veterinary-service structure, published clinic locations, and local dog-health studies can help identify practitioners and possible referral networks. | Equal access to licensed private companion-animal care, current appointment capacity, specialty coverage, service quality, or which practices accept Waggies referrals. Public-sector livestock/animal-health presence is not proof of private pet-care access. | A local catchment with named, currently licensed clinicians and facilities, referral/emergency arrangements, and confirmed service availability. |
| Pet relocation | Airport network, NAQS procedures, destination-country requirements, and published provider routes can reveal potential origin/destination corridors and compliance steps. | Current pet carriage by an airline, cargo acceptance, schedule reliability, live-animal handling quality, route pricing, or Waggies’ partner availability. An airport alone is not a route. | A verified origin-to-destination route with carrier/handler acceptance, current documentation requirements, care/handoff owners, quote inputs, and contingency plan. |

## Geographic screening signals

- **Abuja/FCT:** The 2023 population projection is approximately 3.17 million. A 2018 Gwagwalada study estimated 103,758 dogs in that study area using a human-to-dog ratio; the finding is dated, geographically limited, and not a measure of current paid pet-care demand. Keep Abuja as Waggies’ known operating base, but do not generalize the study to the whole FCT or current service capacity. [NBS Demographic Bulletin 2023](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/182/download/1392/Demographic%20Bulletin%202023.pdf); [Gwagwalada dog-ecology study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28782905/)
- **Lagos and Kano:** NBS projections place both among the most populous states. Lagos has a published dog-ecology study and appears in official airport information; Kano is a northern commercial/transport node with a listed airport. These are reasons to investigate provider, partner, route, and customer-access evidence; they do not prove either state should be Waggies’ next market. [NBS Demographic Bulletin 2023](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/182/download/1392/Demographic%20Bulletin%202023.pdf); [Lagos dog-ecology study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4825384/); [FAAN airports](https://faan.gov.ng/local-airports/)
- **Other states:** A 2024 review cites dog ecology estimates/studies in a limited number of locations, including Abia, Bauchi, Benue, FCT, Kaduna, Kwara, Lagos, Nasarawa, Niger, Osun, and Plateau, with different years and methods. It explicitly reports that only 6 of 37 state/FCT units had relevant dog population/ownership or related data; a cited study location is not proof of present service availability or sufficient customer volume. [Abubakar et al., 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/zph.13084)

## Evidence quality and gaps

| Signal | Confidence for its stated use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| NBS projected population by state | Medium for broad screening; very low for pet-service demand | Official demographic source, but human-population projections do not measure pet ownership, ability to pay, or demand; projections should not be treated as current census counts. |
| Academic dog-ecology studies | Medium for the sampled place and period; low for nationwide transfer | Studies use local samples, differing periods and methods, and often focus on rabies/public health. They do not measure paid care demand. |
| National dog-population extrapolation | Low for commercial sizing | It uses dog-to-human assumptions in a data-sparse setting; the 2024 review itself notes data for only 6/37 state/FCT units. Do not multiply it by a service spend to claim TAM. |
| Veterinary premises/register evidence | Low for current geographic supply | The available national count is from the 2019 evaluation and not state-disaggregated in the cited passage; register freshness was a documented concern. |
| Airport presence | Medium for infrastructure screening; low for pet-route feasibility | FAAN’s airport list proves airport infrastructure, not active flight schedules or acceptance and handling of live animals on a specific route. |
| NAQS published requirements | High for the listed regulatory process; route-specific application still needs confirmation | NAQS is the responsible public authority, but destination-country rules, timing, fees, and operational implementation can vary. Confirm on each case. |

Key gaps to preserve in the strategy:

- No current official state-by-state owned-dog or owned-cat dataset was identified in the sources reviewed.
- Published dog estimates are sparse and not directly comparable; companion-animal ownership is not the same as demand for boarding or veterinary services.
- No reliable public state-level boarding capacity, utilization, clinic appointment availability, private veterinary service mix, or relocation volume series was identified.
- Public route and airport data do not confirm airline pet policies, carrier capacity, prices, or actual Waggies partner access.
- Public sources cannot answer Waggies-specific capacity, staffing, licenses, partner readiness, quality controls, or economics; those require owner-confirmed facts in the operating-guardrails decision.

## Decision-use guidance

Do not choose a national rollout solely from population rank, online visibility, clinic listings, or airports. Use the following evidence sequence for each service/geography cell:

1. Confirm Waggies’ current operating capability and constraints for the service.
2. Verify real providers/partners and service availability in the location or route.
3. Combine public animal-population/health evidence with human population only as explicitly labeled screening proxies.
4. Identify route, safety, regulatory, partner, and service-quality prerequisites.
5. Classify the cell as current, partner-deliverable after verification, or unsupported by evidence; keep unsupported cells out of public availability claims.
6. Set any numerical target only after the baseline and measurement method are approved.

This evidence supports a phased, service-specific coverage decision. It does not yet identify a defensible state-by-state expansion order or establish that Waggies can outperform any provider.
