# Waggies Nigerian competitor and service-coverage evidence map

*Research snapshot: 2026-10-23*

## Purpose and limits

This note extends the first-pass [Waggies Competitive Landscape](waggies-competitive-landscape-2026-10.md) for the Wayfinder decision about Nigerian coverage across boarding, veterinary care, and pet relocation. It uses public provider, directory, university, and government sources. Waggies has no customer base available for interviews or customer-derived evidence, so this report does not use customer interviews, surveys, or fabricated preference data.

This is a map of **discoverable public evidence**, not a census of Nigerian providers, a state-by-state demand estimate, or a verification of operating capacity. A location, route, service page, or directory listing establishes only what that source says. It does not establish current availability, service quality, transaction volume, outcomes, regulatory compliance, or that a service is offered in every nearby state. “No provider found in this scan” means the scan did not find adequate public evidence; it does not mean the area is unserved.

### Locations not established by this evidence map

The scan did not produce enough reliable, first-party evidence to map boarding or pet-relocation providers across every one of Nigeria’s states. For example, an Enugu directory listing and a Kano third-party social listing surfaced, but they are not strong enough on their own to establish current service operations. The absence of a table row for a state is not evidence that it has no providers. A complete inventory needs systematic state-by-state searches, local directory cross-checks, and direct availability checks; those are future work, not conclusions from this snapshot.

## Service-line view

### Boarding

- The strongest discoverable concentration in this scan is Lagos, where multiple provider sites publish boarding offers, booking details, admission requirements, or at least a named facility. Abuja also has multiple advertised providers, including BlissPets and Doggies Dreamland.
- Public boarding specifics vary: House of Pluto exposes dates, feeding schedule, medication, and suite selection in its booking form; Exotic Pet Café publishes facility separation and admission details; Pet Town Africa displays an indicative daily fee. Those are published process/details, not proof of delivered quality or current availability.
- University veterinary teaching hospitals and general veterinary clinics should not be treated as boarding competitors unless they explicitly advertise boarding. A clinic’s inpatient ward is for treatment and is not equivalent to commercial boarding.

### Veterinary care

- Official university sources make public clinical services discoverable outside Abuja and Lagos: Ibadan, Zaria, Ilorin, Nsukka, Sokoto, and historically Abeokuta. Their service models include teaching, referral, ambulatory, and companion-animal care; they may be alternatives or referral partners, but are not necessarily direct commercial competitors.
- Private providers surfaced in Abuja, Lagos, Ibadan, and Port Harcourt. Public pages can identify an advertised location and service; they do not verify licensing, credentials, appointment availability, or case outcomes. Credential claims should be checked against the Veterinary Council of Nigeria before Waggies uses them in comparative marketing.

### Pet relocation

- The clearest formal directory evidence is Move My Pet’s IPATA profile: it lists Lagos primary airport, Abuja secondary airport, in-house import/export, kennel preparation, pickup/delivery, and veterinary services; other activities such as boarding, customs brokerage, freight forwarding, grooming, and long-distance transport are marked as arrangements. IPATA also says member profiles distinguish in-house from arranged services. Membership and declared categories do not certify outcomes or current route availability. [IPATA profile and category key](https://www.ipata.org/member/2367).
- Wastol advertises domestic transport between Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt plus international transport. Zero3 advertises those three hubs and a detailed quote/route experience. These are provider claims; confirm before treating them as active route coverage.
- KOEMAN lists branches in several Nigerian cities and advertises pet relocation, but its public site has limited pet-specific process detail. Broader moving or logistics branches should not be counted as pet-service coverage without confirmation.
- JB-LOIS describes animal airfreight, customs, and import/export consulting and lists contacts in Abuja, Enugu, Lagos, and Port Harcourt. Its page is for a South African freight business, so the Nigerian contact list does not establish a Nigerian pet-relocation branch network or domestic service footprint.
- MyGoToVet appears to center on permit/documentation and veterinary travel support, not confirmed physical transport. Do not aggregate paperwork assistance with door-to-door relocation.
- A Travo.ng article names other providers and routes, but it is a secondary article. Those names should enter a verification queue, not the confirmed inventory, until first-party or authoritative evidence is found.

## Evidence quality and source checks

Use these labels in later decisions:

- **High confidence for stated fact only:** official university or government pages for their published location, mandate, or process; IPATA for its own member listing and the categories the member declares.
- **Medium confidence for advertised offer:** provider-owned service pages, booking pages, addresses, and quote flows. Treat the offer as advertised, not independently verified.
- **Low / provisional:** stale pages, inaccessible pages, third-party directories/social accounts, and self-reported metrics that conflict across the provider’s own pages.

Notable checks:

- Zero3Logistics’ homepage states “500+ pets moved,” while its About page in the earlier baseline places the same 500+ milestone in 2029 after its stated 2026 founding. Do not cite those metrics as proof of scale or service performance until reconciled. [Homepage](https://zero3logistics.com/), [About page](https://zero3logistics.com/about/).
- House of Pluto’s public booking page is dated with a 2023 footer and was last crawled more than a year before this snapshot. It is a lead, not current proof of availability.
- Dr. Tessy’s public service page appeared in search, but direct retrieval returned a gateway error on the research date. The service claim is provisional pending a successful re-check.
- Some university sources provide strong institutional identity but do not publish hours, fees, appointment requirements, or whether a specific clinic currently accepts external companion-animal cases. Confirm service access directly before recommending them as alternatives or partners.
- Government animal-quarantine pages establish regulatory responsibilities, not a private provider’s authorization. NAQS says it performs health inspection for imports/exports of live animals, including dogs and cats. Check the current route and destination requirements before publishing any travel instruction. [NAQS animal quarantine](https://naqs.gov.ng/animal/), [NAQS](https://www.naqs.gov.ng/).

## Implications for the coverage decision

1. **“All Nigeria” cannot be translated into one single radius or promise.** Boarding is a location-bound service; veterinary care may be local, referral-based, or ambulatory; relocation may be route-based and partner-dependent. Define geography separately for each line.
2. **The public evidence currently supports a shortlist of visible clusters, not a national demand ranking.** Lagos and Abuja have the richest visible private-provider and pet-travel claims in this scan. Other cities have institutional veterinary evidence and scattered provider claims. That difference may reflect web visibility, not actual market size or demand.
3. **Use layered coverage hypotheses rather than declaring all states served:** (a) direct local delivery, (b) verified partner or referral reach, (c) route/request-based service subject to confirmation, and (d) not yet verified. Keep “not yet verified” distinct from “unserved.”
4. **Separate logistics reach from care access.** A national pet transport route does not establish boarding or clinical service availability in the destination. Likewise, a university hospital’s regional referral mandate does not establish Waggies partner coverage.
5. **The next owner decision needs Waggies’ operating facts.** Public competitor research cannot tell us Waggies’ facility capacity, vet staffing/credentials, partner agreements, service hours, or routes. The growth model should wait for the owner-confirmed operating-guardrails ticket and should not turn aspirational expansion into a public service claim.
